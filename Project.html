Diet

1. Balance (The Right Ratios): A dog cannot survive on just meat or just grains. A healthy diet requires a precise balance of proteins, healthy fats, complex carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals. You don't necessarily have to balance every single individual meal perfectly, but the nutrients must balance out over time to prevent organ deficiency or toxicity. 2. Moderation (Calorie & Weight Control): Obesity is one of the leading threats to a dog's lifespan. Moderation means feeding strictly according to your dog's ideal target weight and activity level rather than letting them overeat. It also means enforcing the 10% treat rule so that snacks don't ruin their base metabolism
3. Life Stage Calibration (Age Matters): A dog's nutritional needs change drastically over their life. Puppies require high-protein, calorie-dense foods to build growing bones. Adults require lower-calorie maintenance food. Seniors need highly digestible, joint-supporting formulas that protect their slowing organs. Feeding the wrong stage can lead to developmental or weight issues. 4. Hydration (The Foundation): Water is the most important nutrient in a dog's body, which is made up of about 70% water. Even a minor 10% drop in hydration can cause severe illness. Whether your dog eats dry kibble or wet food, they must have constant access to fresh water to regulate their body temperature, aid digestion, and flush metabolic waste.
